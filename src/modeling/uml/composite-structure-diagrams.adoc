= Composite structure diagrams

A composite structure diagram shows the internal structure of a classifier
(such as a class, component, or collaboration) and the collaborations that this
structure makes possible. It depicts the runtime instances of interconnected
elements collaborating over communication links to achieve a common objective.

Composite structure diagrams are structure diagrams introduced in UML 2.0.
They can be used to show:

* The internal structure of a classifier — internal structure diagram.
* Classifier interactions with the environment through ports.
* The behavior of a collaboration — collaboration use diagram.

== Purpose

Composite structure diagrams are used to:

* Decompose a class, component, or use case into its internal parts.
* Show the points of interaction (ports) that a component has with other parts
  of the system.
* Model collaborations between instances at runtime.
* Describe design patterns and their application in a specific context.
* Visualize the internal architecture of complex components.

== Notation

Composite structure diagrams use the following elements:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Element | Notation

| Structured classifier
| A class or component rectangle — represents a classifier whose internal
  structure is being shown.

| Part
| A rectangle within the structured classifier, labeled with `roleName:
  Classifier`. Represents a role played at runtime by an instance.

| Port
| A small square on the boundary of a classifier — an interaction point
  through which the classifier communicates with its environment or internal
  parts.

| Connector
| A line connecting parts, ports, or a combination — binds entities together
  for runtime interaction.

| Collaboration
| A dashed oval containing roles that instances can play in the
  collaboration.

| Collaboration use
| A dashed oval with dashed lines (role bindings) to the parts — represents
  one particular use of a collaboration pattern.
|===

=== Structured classifier

A *structured classifier* is a class or component whose behavior can be
described through interactions between its parts. An *encapsulated classifier*
is a structured classifier that contains ports.

=== Part

A *part* represents a role played at runtime by one instance of a classifier
or by a collection of instances. The part may name only the role, it may name
an abstract superclass, or it may name a specific concrete class. A part can
include a multiplicity factor (e.g., `[0..*]`).

=== Port

A *port* is an interaction point on the boundary of a classifier. Ports can
optionally specify the services they provide and the services they require from
other parts of the system. A port is drawn as a small square straddling the
classifier's boundary, regardless of its visibility.

Like any other property, a port has a visibility (`+` public, `-` private,
`#` protected, `~` package), shown as a prefix on its name. A public port is
visible to, and can be interacted with by, the classifier's environment; a
private or protected port is restricted to internal use.

Each port has a type and is labeled with a name. Ports may contain a
multiplicity factor (e.g., `[3]`).

Ports can either delegate received requests to internal parts, or deliver them
directly to the behavior of the structured classifier.

=== Connector

A *connector* binds two or more entities together, allowing them to interact
at runtime. It is shown as a line between some combination of parts, ports, and
structured classifiers. There are two named kinds:

* **Assembly connector** — wires one part's provided interface to another
  part's required interface, drawn as the same ball-and-socket ("keyhole")
  symbol used in component diagrams.
* **Delegation connector** — forwards requests arriving at a port on the
  structured classifier's boundary to a port on one of its internal parts (or
  vice versa for outgoing requests). Drawn as a line with an open arrowhead,
  connecting an outer port to an inner port or part.

[plantuml]
....
class Car {
  port engineControl
}

Car *-- "1" Engine
Car *-- "1" FuelSystem

Engine -( IFuel
FuelSystem -- IFuel

Car::engineControl --> Engine : delegation
....

Here, `Engine` and `FuelSystem` are wired together with an assembly
connector via the `IFuel` interface, while `Car`'s external `engineControl`
port delegates to `Engine` internally.

=== Collaboration

A *collaboration* describes the relevant aspects of the cooperation of a set
of instances by identifying the specific roles that the instances will play.
It is shown as a dashed oval containing roles. Collaborations are more abstract
than structured classifiers — roles are often typed by interfaces rather than
concrete classes.

=== Collaboration use

A *collaboration use* represents one particular application of a collaboration
pattern to a specific situation. It binds specific entities from a given
context to the roles of the collaboration. It is shown as a dashed ellipse with
dashed lines (role bindings) to the parts.

== Relationship to other diagrams

Composite structure diagrams are closely related to:

* **Class diagrams** — composite structure diagrams decompose classes into
  their internal parts, ports, and connectors.
* **Component diagrams** — components are structured classifiers; composite
  structure diagrams can show their internal wiring.
* **Object role model (ORM) diagrams** — similar in notation, but ORM diagrams
  focus on detailed relationships between entities while composite structure
  diagrams focus on collaborations.
* **Sequence diagrams** — sequence diagrams are often preferred for exploring
  collaborations because of their more robust notation and wider developer
  familiarity.

== Applications

* Modeling the internal architecture of complex components and subsystems.
* Designing the wiring of enterprise application servers (e.g., Tomcat, JBoss).
* Describing design patterns (e.g., Observer pattern) and their application.
* Modeling the internal structure of bank ATMs, medical devices, and other
  embedded systems.
* Documenting the collaboration between objects in a specific use case
  realization.

== Advantages

* Provides a detailed view of the internal structure of classifiers.
* Clearly shows how parts interact through ports and connectors.
* Useful for modeling component-based and service-oriented architectures.
* Supports both structural decomposition and behavioral collaboration views.

== Limitations

* Composite structure diagrams are a niche diagram type — they are not widely
  used in practice.
* Many UML modeling tools have limited support for composite structure
  diagrams.
* For exploring collaborations, sequence diagrams are often more practical
  and more widely understood.
* The notation can be complex and may require significant training to use
  effectively.
