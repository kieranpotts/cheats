= Bash: Brace expansion

Brace expansion generates every permutation of a comma-separated list, or a
range, and happens BEFORE globbing or variable expansion. Unlike globs, it does
not need to match anything that exists on the filesystem.

[source,bash]
----
echo file{1,2,3}.txt
# file1.txt file2.txt file3.txt

echo {a,b}{1,2}
# a1 a2 b1 b2

mkdir -p project/{src,test,docs}
# Creates project/src, project/test, and project/docs.
----

Ranges are written with `..` and work for both numbers and letters.

[source,bash]
----
echo {1..5}
# 1 2 3 4 5

echo {5..1}
# 5 4 3 2 1

echo {a..e}
# a b c d e

# Zero-padded ranges.
echo {01..10}
# 01 02 03 04 05 06 07 08 09 10

# Ranges with a step (Bash 4+).
echo {0..20..5}
# 0 5 10 15 20
----

Because brace expansion happens independently of the filesystem, it's commonly
used to build lists of arguments or filenames that don't exist yet.

[source,bash]
----
touch backup-{2024,2025,2026}.tar.gz
----

Don't confuse `{1..9}` (brace expansion) with `[1-9]` (a glob/regex character
class) – they look similar but are unrelated features evaluated at different
times.
